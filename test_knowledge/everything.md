Event Overview
PROCOM’26 is Pakistan’s premier annual technology festival, hosted by FAST National University of Computer and Emerging Sciences (FAST‑NUCES), Karachi.
It brings together students, professionals, and innovators from across the country to compete, create, and connect through technology and innovation.
The event has been running since 1998, making it one of the longest‑standing tech events in Pakistan.
PROCOM’26 provides a platform for technical excellence, creative problem solving, and industry-academia engagement.
The festival typically includes competitions, workshops, seminars, and networking opportunities designed to help participants sharpen skills and meet professionals.
PROCOM’26 spans multiple domains such as Computer Science, Artificial Intelligence, Electrical Engineering, Business, and interdisciplinary challenges.
The event attracts thousands of students and industry partners, fostering a collaborative environment for learning, innovation, and career growth.

The Multiverse Lore — PROCOM’26 Computer Science Competitions
Core Meaning of the Multiverse
At its core,The Multiverse is a story about fundamental conflicts and philosophical tension:
Control versus Free Will  
Forced balance versus natural chaos  
Systems treating lives as variables rather than individuals  
A single being unknowingly becoming the key to all of reality  
A reset that saves existence but permanently costs innocence and individuality  
The narrative uses science-fiction and computing metaphors such as gateways, signatures, glitches, engines, and system locks to mirror concepts from computer science, including:
Competitive programming  
Debugging and fault isolation  
System failures and crashes  
Logical locks and constraints  
Irreversible operations  
This alignment makes the lore deeply integrated with the theme of technology and computer science competitions.
Multiversal Structure (Cosmology)
The OmniVerse
The highest layer of existence.
Exists outside normal timelines and causality.
Contains all universes and multiverses.
Home of OmniVerse Kiro, the Prime Anchor.
Functions like the root process or kernel of reality.
The Multiverse
A collection of interconnected universes linked through gateways.
Each universe:
Has its own physical and logical rules.
Contains its own version of Kiro.
Represents a distinct system state or conceptual domain.
The Multiversal Core — Erevos-901 (The Red Star)
The central hub of the entire multiverse.
All gateways ultimately connect to Erevos-901.
Can only be triggered directly in physical space, not through portals.
Acts as a supernode or root server of reality.
Incorrect triggering results in The Glitch, a catastrophic system violation.

Electrical Engineering Competitions(EE) – 11 February 2026
On 11 February 2026, FAST NUCES Karachi will host a series of Electrical Engineering competitions. Robo Soccer is a one-on-one robotic match where each team must defend their goal while attempting to score on the opponent’s side. Matches follow a knockout format, with 50% of teams eliminated after each round, and a golden goal decides the winner in case of a draw. Similarly, Robo Sumo features autonomous or remote-controlled robots competing to push their opponents out of a circular or rectangular ring, following the same knockout format with 50% elimination per round and golden goal resolution for draws.

In Robo War (Light Weight), teams design robots that are both agile and offensive, capable of strong attacks, defense, and fast movement. Matches may have a time limit or end with a knockout, with winners determined by judges based on points scored. Knockout rounds follow the same 50% elimination rule, with draws resolved by golden goal. Line Following Robot (LFR) challenges teams to build fully autonomous robots that navigate a predefined track by following a visible line through curves, intersections, and obstacles, completing the course in the shortest time possible. LFR matches are one-on-one knockout rounds with the same elimination and golden goal rules.

The Speed Soldering competition tests electronics assembly skills in three rounds. Round 1 runs from 10:00 to 10:30 and is a paper-based MCQ test, eliminating 30% of teams. Round 2, from 11:00 to 12:00, is a software-based simulation, with 50% elimination. Round 3, from 14:00 to 15:30, is a hardware round where participants solder given components on a vero board, and winners are decided based on accuracy and task completion time.

Digital Logic Design (DLD) challenges participants to design, implement, and test complex digital circuits using logic gates, flip-flops, adders, and counters. Round 1 takes place from 10:00 to 10:30 as a paper-based MCQ test, Round 2 runs from 11:00 to 12:00 with paper-based questions, and Round 3 occurs from 14:00 to 15:30 with hardware-based circuit design. Winners are determined by judges based on accuracy and completion time.

In Robo Parkour, robots navigate a matrix or grid arena to collect balls at specific coordinates based on pre-assigned instructions. Rounds follow a knockout format with 50% elimination per round, and the final round determines the winner.

The schedule for the events is as follows: LFR Round 1 and Robo Soccer Round 1 are from 10:00 to 11:00, followed by LFR Round 2 and Robo Soccer Round 2 from 11:00 to 12:00, with their finals from 12:00 to 12:30. Robo War Round 1 and Robo Parkour Round 1 run from 14:00 to 14:45, Round 2 from 14:45 to 15:30, and the finals from 15:30 to 16:00. Robo Sumo rounds occur from 14:00 to 15:00 for Round 1, 15:00 to 15:30 for Round 2, and 15:30 to 16:00 for the final. Speed Soldering and DLD rounds are staggered similarly: 10:00 to 10:30 for Round 1, 11:00 to 12:00 for Round 2, and 14:00 to 15:30 for Round 3, with 30-minute buffers in between rounds.

Across all EE competitions, 50% elimination per round applies to knockout events. Participants must follow scheduled times strictly, and teams may be disqualified for misconduct, sabotage, or rule violations. Judges’ decisions are final.

How do I register for a competition?
Registration is through the official event portal. Each competition may have separate registration requirements and fees.
Can I participate in more than one competition?
Yes, but participants must manage timing conflicts. Some competitions may require exclusive participation (e.g., timed rounds).
Are there team limits?
Team sizes vary by competition: Business competitions (1–5), CS competitions (1–3), EE competitions (1–3), general competitions may be individual or team-based as specified.
Competition Rules
Is internet access allowed?
It depends on the competition. Most CS and CTF competitions do not allow external internet usage except for specific platforms like VJudge or GitHub. Business competitions allow no external tools or internet browsing. General and EE competitions may allow internet for documentation only if mentioned.
Are mobile phones allowed?
Mobile phones are strictly prohibited during competitions unless explicitly stated. Phones may need to be submitted to coordinators.
